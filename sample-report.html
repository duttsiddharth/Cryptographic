Mosca's inequality is already breached

Data must stay confidential for 25 years and migration is estimated at 4 years, against 9 years before a cryptographically relevant quantum computer is assumed to exist (2035). 25 + 4 = 29, which exceeds 9 by 20 years.

Anything encrypted today with a quantum-broken algorithm should be treated as already disclosed to any adversary recording traffic. Migration planning cannot wait for a hardware announcement.

This organisation is classified as critical information infrastructure, so the binding deadline is 2027. At an estimated 4-year migration, work should already have begun, in 2023. The remaining window is shorter than the migration it has to hold.

What to do, in order

  • Fix 6 classically broken findings first. These are exploitable today and do not depend on any quantum assumption. They are also the easiest budget to obtain, because the risk is not hypothetical.
  • Schedule 11 high-priority replacements. These use algorithms that Shor's algorithm breaks outright. Plan hybrid deployment — classical alongside post-quantum — rather than a cutover.
  • Make the inventory continuous. A cryptographic BOM generated once is stale within a sprint. Run this in CI and fail the build on a new quantum-broken dependency, the way a software BOM is already handled.
  • Write crypto-agility into the architecture. The specific algorithm matters less than whether it can be swapped without a rebuild. Version the algorithm identifier in every protocol and stored format now.
  • Put PQC clauses in procurement. Every contract signed before 2027 should require the vendor to state a post-quantum roadmap and supply a cryptographic bill of materials.
  • Register the programme with the sector regulator. As critical information infrastructure, evidence of a phased plan will be expected, not just an end state.
